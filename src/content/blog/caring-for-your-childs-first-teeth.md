Parents often ask us, "Is it too early?" about almost everything related to their child's teeth — brushing, first visits, even flossing. The short answer: it's rarely too early. Here's a simple timeline we share with every new parent.

## Before the first tooth even appears

Wipe your baby's gums gently with a clean, damp washcloth after feedings. This gets them comfortable with the sensation of oral care long before brushing begins, and helps clear away sugars from milk.

## When the first tooth erupts

As soon as that first tooth appears (usually between 6–10 months), start brushing with:

- A **soft, infant-sized toothbrush**
- A **rice-grain-sized smear** of fluoride toothpaste (not a full pea-sized amount yet)
- Gentle circular motions, twice a day

## The "first birthday, first visit" rule

The Indian Academy of Pediatric Dentistry and international guidelines agree: your child's first dental visit should happen **by their first birthday**, or within six months of the first tooth appearing — whichever comes first.

This isn't about treatment. It's about your child meeting the clinic in a low-pressure way, and about us catching early feeding or alignment patterns while they're easiest to guide.

## Making visits something kids actually enjoy

At Oracle Dental Care, our approach with young patients is built around three things:

1. **No surprises.** We show, explain, and let them touch instruments before we use them ("tell-show-do").
2. **Their pace.** A first visit might just be a chair ride and a smile count — and that's a complete success.
3. **Positive language.** We avoid words like "hurt," "shot," or "drill" — kids remember words more than sensations.

## Common myths we hear from parents

**"Baby teeth don't matter, they'll fall out anyway."**
Baby teeth hold space for adult teeth, support speech development, and matter enormously for nutrition and confidence. Cavities in baby teeth are still infections, and still need treatment.

**"My child cried, so the dentist was 'bad' for them."**
A single stressful visit doesn't undo good habits — but avoiding the dentist because of one hard visit often does. We're happy to go slowly across multiple short visits if that's what a child needs.
