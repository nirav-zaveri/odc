Almost every patient who comes in about crooked teeth asks the same question within the first two minutes: *"Can I get the invisible ones?"*

Sometimes the answer is yes, and aligners are genuinely the better choice. Sometimes fixed braces will give a better result in less time for less money. The honest answer depends on your teeth — not on which option sounds more appealing.

## The side-by-side comparison

![Braces versus clear aligners compared across visibility, treatment time, removability, complex corrections, and discipline required](/images/blog/braces-vs-aligners.svg)

## What braces do better

**Complex movements.** If teeth need rotating, significantly moving, or if there is a substantial bite problem, fixed braces have more mechanical control. There are corrections that aligners simply cannot achieve predictably.

**They work whether or not you remember.** Braces are attached. They apply force twenty-four hours a day without requiring anything from you. For teenagers especially, this matters more than most parents expect.

**Cost.** Fixed braces are usually the more affordable option for equivalent correction.

## What aligners do better

**Appearance during treatment.** This is the obvious one, and for adults in client-facing work it is often decisive.

**Hygiene.** You take them out to brush and floss normally. With braces, cleaning around brackets and wires takes real effort, and patients who do not manage it can finish treatment with straight teeth and new white marks on the enamel.

**Comfort and eating.** No wires to catch, no food restrictions — you remove them to eat.

**Fewer emergency visits.** No brackets to come loose.

## The honest catch with aligners

Aligners only work while you are wearing them, and they need **20–22 hours a day**. That means you take them out to eat and to brush, and they go straight back in.

In practice, this is where aligner treatment succeeds or fails. Patients who leave them out through a long dinner, forget them overnight, or take them out whenever they are self-conscious end up extending treatment by months. If you know you will struggle with this, braces are not a downgrade — they are the more reliable choice for you.

## What about children and teenagers?

For younger patients we consider a third factor: timing. Some bite problems are far easier to correct while the jaw is still growing. This is why we recommend an orthodontic assessment around ages 8–10, even if no treatment starts then — it lets us intervene at the right moment rather than the convenient one.

## Retainers: the part nobody mentions

Whichever route you take, teeth drift back toward their original position. This is normal biology, not treatment failure.

You will need a retainer afterwards — usually nightly at first, then a few nights a week long-term. Patients who stop wearing retainers entirely often see noticeable relapse within a couple of years. Please factor this into your decision: orthodontic treatment is not quite "finished" the day the braces come off.

## How to decide

Book a consultation and ask for both options to be assessed. A good orthodontic plan starts with an X-ray and a look at your bite — not with a preference. We will tell you plainly if aligners will not give you the result you want, and equally plainly if they will work beautifully.
