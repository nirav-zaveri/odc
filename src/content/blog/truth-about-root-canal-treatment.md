If I had to pick the single most misunderstood procedure in dentistry, it would be root canal treatment (RCT) without question. Almost every patient walks in expecting the worst — and almost every patient walks out surprised at how straightforward it was.

## Where the fear comes from

Decades ago, before modern anesthesia and rotary instruments, root canal treatment genuinely was a difficult procedure. That reputation stuck around long after the technology — and the experience — changed completely.

## What actually causes the pain, before treatment

The pain that sends people searching "root canal near me" at 2 AM isn't caused by the treatment — it's caused by the infected or inflamed nerve *inside* the tooth. That pain exists whether or not you get treated. Root canal treatment removes the source of that pain; it doesn't create it.

## What modern RCT actually involves

1. **Numbing the area** — thoroughly, before anything else begins.
2. **Removing the infected pulp** from inside the tooth using fine rotary instruments.
3. **Cleaning and shaping** the canal to remove bacteria.
4. **Sealing the space** so infection can't return.
5. **Restoring the tooth** — usually with a filling or crown — so it's protected long-term.

With today's rotary systems, most straightforward cases are completed comfortably in a **single sitting**.

## Why saving the natural tooth matters

An extracted tooth needs to be replaced — with an implant, a bridge, or a partial denture — all of which cost more, take longer, and never quite replicate a natural tooth. Root canal treatment, when the tooth is a good candidate, is almost always the option that preserves the most function for the least intervention.

## The bottom line

If you're avoiding a dental visit because you're worried it might mean "the dreaded root canal," please don't. The procedure you're picturing hasn't existed in dentistry for a long time — and the tooth pain you're avoiding will not go away on its own.
