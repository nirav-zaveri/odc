Few topics generate more questions — and more misinformation — than teeth whitening. Here's a straightforward, no-hype breakdown.

## First: understand what "yellowing" actually is

Teeth darken for two very different reasons, and the treatment depends entirely on which one applies to you:

- **Surface (extrinsic) staining** — from coffee, tea, turmeric, tobacco, or red wine sitting on enamel. This responds well to whitening.
- **Internal (intrinsic) discoloration** — from genetics, certain medications, or ageing enamel becoming thinner. This needs a different approach, like veneers, rather than whitening alone.

A quick consultation tells us which one you're dealing with, so you're not paying for a treatment that was never going to work.

## What actually works

### In-clinic professional whitening
Uses a controlled, higher-concentration gel applied under supervision, often activated with light. Results are visible immediately and it's the most predictable option for an event or a fast result.

### Take-home whitening trays (dentist-supervised)
Custom-fitted trays with a professional-grade gel you use at home over 1–2 weeks. Slower than in-clinic whitening, but excellent, even results with less sensitivity for most people.

### Consistent oral hygiene
Sounds unglamorous, but reducing surface staining at the source — good brushing technique, and rinsing after tea/coffee — meaningfully slows how fast teeth re-stain after any whitening treatment.

## What doesn't work (or actively causes harm)

- **Charcoal powders/toothpaste** — abrasive enough to wear away enamel over time, which can make teeth look *more* yellow long-term as the darker dentin layer underneath becomes visible.
- **Lemon juice or baking soda scrubs** — acidic and abrasive; a fast way to damage enamel permanently for a barely-there result.
- **Unregulated whitening pens/strips** — concentration and fit aren't controlled, so results are inconsistent and gum irritation is common.

## A note on sensitivity

Some temporary sensitivity to hot/cold is normal after professional whitening and usually resolves within a couple of days. If you already have sensitive teeth, tell us before treatment — we can adjust the approach so whitening stays comfortable.

## Our honest recommendation

If your goal is a noticeably brighter smile without damaging your enamel, professional whitening — in-clinic or supervised take-home — is simply the only method with predictable, dentist-verified results.
